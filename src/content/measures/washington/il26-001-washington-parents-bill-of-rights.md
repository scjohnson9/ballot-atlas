The measure would restore the original 2024 Parents' Bill of Rights (Initiative 2081) as passed with strong bipartisan support, re-enacting parental rights that were significantly weakened by the Legislature through HB 1296 in 2025—including the right to review classroom materials in advance, opt out of sensitive classroom content, and be notified about school-based medical and mental health services provided to their children.

  - question: What would IL26-001 change about current Washington law?
    answer: |-
      [IL26-001](https://www2.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/initiatives/finaltext_3264.pdf?_gl=1*e9rwk0*_ga*NzU2NzQ3OTY1LjE3NDk4MjUxMzI.*_ga_7B08VE04WV*czE3NTAxODc4MzMkbzgkZzEkdDE3NTAxODc4NDAkajUzJGwwJGgw*_ga_X6SDF160YQ*czE3NTAxODc4MzMkbzgkZzEkdDE3NTAxODc4NDAkajUzJGwwJGgw) is unique in the sense that it provides voters with an opportunity to reinstate parental rights instituted by [I-2081 (2024)](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2023-24/Pdf/Initiatives/Initiatives/INITIATIVE%202081.pdf?q=20260910133212). Those rights were modified by the Washington state legislature in [HB 1296 (2025)](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Pdf/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1296-S.SL.pdf?q=20260910133253). 

      If voters approve IL26-001, some reinstated parental rights would include: 

      - The right to ["review materials and student records,"](https://www.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/initiatives/ballottitleletter_3264.pdf) 
      - The right ["to receive prior notification when medical services are being offered to their child,"](https://www2.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/initiatives/finaltext_3264.pdf?_gl=1*e9rwk0*_ga*NzU2NzQ3OTY1LjE3NDk4MjUxMzI.*_ga_7B08VE04WV*czE3NTAxODc4MzMkbzgkZzEkdDE3NTAxODc4NDAkajUzJGwwJGgw*_ga_X6SDF160YQ*czE3NTAxODc4MzMkbzgkZzEkdDE3NTAxODc4NDAkajUzJGwwJGgw) and
      - "the option to opt their child out of any... student engagements that include questions" about their "sexual experiences or attractions;" religion, or mental health.

      Additionally, if voters approve IL26-001, certain modifications from HB 1296 would be reversed—including those which made it more difficult for parents to view their child's student records or have advance notice of medical services.
